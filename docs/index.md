## What you will be able to do

The course is organised around six capabilities, not around chapters to be covered.
By the end you can:

1. **Summarise data honestly** — pick the summary that matches the shape of the data,
   and say what the summary hides.
2. **Compute and interpret probability** — including conditional probability,
   independence, and a Bayes update done in both directions.
3. **Choose a distribution from the mechanism** that generated the data, and check the
   choice against observed counts.
4. **Reason about sampling variability** — standard errors, the Central Limit Theorem,
   confidence intervals, and what "95 % confidence" actually promises.
5. **Design and read a test** — stated hypotheses, the correct reference distribution,
   p-values, Type I and II error, power, and multiple-group comparisons.
6. **Fit, diagnose and report a model** — regression with its assumptions checked, an
   effect size, an interval, and a written statement of what the data cannot support.

The full list, mapped to modules and assessment weights, is in the
[syllabus](syllabus.md#course-learning-outcomes).

## How this course teaches

<div class="psds-grid psds-grid--2" markdown>

<div class="psds-card psds-card--plain" markdown>
### Understanding before formulas

Every result is introduced as an idea, then a picture, then symbols, then a
derivation. The Central Limit Theorem, for instance, is taught by repeated
sampling before it is stated formally — [try the lab](labs/clt.md).
</div>

<div class="psds-card psds-card--plain" markdown>
### Simulation as a method

Fourteen [interactive laboratories](labs/index.md) — random events, conditional
probability, distributions, the law of large numbers, the CLT, sampling, interval
coverage, p-values, regression, Bayes updating, power and ANOVA — each run through
the same protocol: theory, experiment, expected result, code, output, comparison,
and an explanation of the gap.
</div>

<div class="psds-card psds-card--plain" markdown>
### Mathematics that you can compute

Every major topic connects to a Python practical, and every practical shows the
manual calculation *and* the code, so you can see what the function does rather
than trust it.
</div>

<div class="psds-card psds-card--plain" markdown>
### Assessment you can trust

Each module ends with a quiz and explained answers; each part ends with exercises
at three difficulty levels and full solutions. The [assessment scheme](syllabus.md#assessment)
mirrors a taught programme.
</div>

</div>
