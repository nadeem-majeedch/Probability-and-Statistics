# Part I — Foundations

**Modules M01–M04 · 44 study hours · Beginner · No prerequisites**

---

## Why this part exists

Statistics is written in the language of sums, integrals and functions, and it is
applied to data whose type determines which summary is even legal. Part I builds both
halves of that foundation before any probability appears — and it begins with a module
that answers the question students rarely ask out loud: what is statistics *for*?

The order is deliberate. M01 supplies the vocabulary and the method of study. M02
rebuilds the mathematics you will actually use, at exactly the depth later modules
require. M03 establishes what a variable is, how data fails, and how to summarise it
honestly. M04 makes the consequences visible: a summary hides structure, and a figure
reveals it.

Students often want to skip to probability. The cost of doing so is precise: without
M03, the word "variance" in M08 is a formula rather than a quantity you have computed
by hand on real numbers.

## What you will be able to do

- Translate a question about data into symbols, and a formula back into a sentence.
- Classify any variable by type and scale, and state what arithmetic each permits.
- Compute and interpret the mean, median, standard deviation, IQR and z-scores, and
  defend the choice you made.
- Recognise skew, outliers and Simpson's paradox, and explain what each does to a
  conclusion.
- Produce an exploratory analysis whose figures answer the question and whose captions
  state the finding.

## Common misconceptions this part removes

| Misconception | Corrected in |
|---|---|
| "The mean is the middle value" | M03 |
| "A bigger sample is a better sample" | M01 |
| "An outlier is an error" | M03 |
| "Correlation needs no design to be meaningful" | M01 |
| "Ordinal labels can always be averaged" | M01, M03 |
| "A histogram's shape is a property of the data alone" | M04 |
