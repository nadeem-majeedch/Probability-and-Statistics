## A revision method that works

1. **Reconstruct, do not reread.** Close the page and write the formula from memory. Rereading
   feels productive and produces almost no retention.
2. **Work one example per formula by hand.** Numbers expose false understanding faster than
   proofs do.
3. **Explain each idea in one sentence, out loud.** If the sentence needs a formula to survive,
   you have memorised, not understood.
4. **Do the past-paper questions under time.** Untimed practice overestimates performance.
5. **Keep an error log.** Every mistake goes in one place with its cause: misread, misremembered,
   misapplied, or arithmetic. The log tells you what to revise; the syllabus does not.

## What to prioritise when time is short

Sampling distributions, confidence intervals and hypothesis testing carry the most marks and the
most conceptual traps. Distributions and expectation are the vocabulary everything else uses. If
you can do those three areas fluently, the rest follows quickly; if you cannot, memorising
Regression output will not save you.
