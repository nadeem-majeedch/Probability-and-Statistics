# Licence and citation

**Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–ShareAlike 4.0 International**
licence (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).

The full legal text is at
<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode>.

In summary, you may:

- **Share** — copy and redistribute the material in any medium or format.
- **Adapt** — remix, transform and build upon it.

provided that you:

- **Attribute** — credit the author, link to the licence, and indicate whether you
  made changes.
- **Do not use it commercially.**
- **Share alike** — distribute your contributions under the same licence.
- Add no legal or technological restrictions that prevent others from doing
  anything the licence permits.

You may reuse the code in your own teaching, research or commercial work, provided
the copyright notice is retained.

## How to cite

If you use this course in teaching, research or writing, please cite it as:

> Majeed, M. N. (2026). *Probability and Statistics for Data Science*.
> Department of Data Science (PUCIT), University of the Punjab, Lahore.
> https://github.com/nadeem-majeedch/Probability-and-Statistics

A machine-readable `CITATION.cff` file is included in the repository, so GitHub and
reference managers can import the citation automatically.

## Datasets and third-party material

Every dataset used in the course is openly licensed, and its provenance is recorded
where it is used:

| Recorded | Why |
|---|---|
| Source and URL | so you can verify the data |
| Retrieval date | because datasets change |
| Licence | so you know what you may do with it |
| Shape and variables | so you know what you are loading |

Third-party datasets remain under their original licences; nothing in this
repository's licence overrides them. Where a figure or passage follows a specific
published source, the source is cited in the module and listed under "Further
reading".

Data that was generated for teaching purposes is labelled **synthetic** wherever it
appears — in the page, in the figure caption and in the notebook header. Synthetic
data is never presented as an observation.

## What this site does not collect

- No analytics, no trackers, no advertising, no third-party pixels.
- No cookies for tracking. Progress is stored in your browser's `localStorage` and
  never transmitted.
- No accounts and no personal data.
- No external services are called except the CDNs that serve the mathematics and
  diagram renderers (see below).

**Third-party requests, disclosed.** The site loads MathJax and Mermaid from public
CDNs to render equations and diagrams, and queries GitHub's public API once to show
this repository's latest release in the header. Those requests necessarily reveal
your IP address to those services, as any web request does. The interactive labs
themselves load nothing — they are entirely local JavaScript, and they work with no
network connection at all once the page has loaded.

## Accessibility

The site targets WCAG 2.2 AA. Every figure has a text description, every display
equation that carries an argument is followed by a plain-language reading, controls
are keyboard operable, and colour is never the only carrier of meaning. If you meet
a barrier, please report it through the issue tracker.

## Corrections

Errors are corrected in the open, with a commit that names the module and section.
Nothing is corrected silently. To report a problem:

<https://github.com/nadeem-majeedch/Probability-and-Statistics/issues>

## Figures

All diagrams and statistical figures in the chapters are original work, generated from
Python by `tools/make_figures.py` and released under the same CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence as the
text. No third-party images, stock photographs or externally sourced charts are used, so no
separate image attribution is required. The set covers counting trees, descriptive
statistics, visual encoding, discrete and continuous distributions, joint densities,
sampling distributions, the central limit theorem, confidence intervals, hypothesis
tests, correlation and regression, ANOVA and Bayesian updating; each figure can be
regenerated byte-for-byte with `python tools/make_figures.py`, and
`python tools/make_figures.py --check` verifies that every figure referenced by a
chapter exists and that no file is orphaned. Reusing a figure follows the same terms as quoting
the text: attribute the author, link to the licence, and share alike.
